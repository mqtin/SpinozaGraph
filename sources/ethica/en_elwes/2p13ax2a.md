When a body in motion impinges on another body at rest, which it is unable to
move, it recoils, in order to continue its motion, and the angle made by the
line of motion in the recoil and the plane of the body at rest, whereon the
moving body has impinged, will be equal to the angle formed by the line of
motion of incidence and the same plane.

So far we have been speaking only of the most simple bodies, which are only
distinguished one from the other by motion and rest, quickness and slowness. We
now pass on to compound bodies.
